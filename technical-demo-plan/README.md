# Technical demo plan

Before a technical discovery or demo meeting, the account executive and solutions engineer get an attendee brief, a demo sequence built around the buyer's stated workflow from approved scenarios, and open questions. Requests nobody has verified stay questions.

*Workflow template · Software technology · Sales · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/technical-demo-plan/)*

## The outcome

The two people going into the meeting share one page. It says who will be in the room and what each has said, lists the steps of the buyer's own working day as the buyer described them, and proposes a running order of demo scenarios your company has already approved, one scenario for each step it can honestly show. Every request the buyer made is matched to an approved scenario or held as a question.

Nothing is stretched. A feature that exists only on the roadmap is kept out of the sequence and out of the promises. A request nobody has checked is not answered from memory; it becomes a question for the solutions engineer to confirm before the meeting and one for the buyer to explain in it. Polite interest from the buyer is not recorded as a commitment.

## Who starts the work, and when

The account executive or the solutions engineer starts it as soon as a technical discovery or demo meeting is booked, and again if the attendees, the agreed agenda or the scenario list change. Both review the plan together before the meeting. They choose the agenda, decide what is shown and confirm that each scenario can be run as described.

## What they supply

- The meeting details: date, length, format, the invited people with their titles where known, and who attends from your side.
- Notes from earlier calls, each dated.
- The agenda already agreed with the buyer, if there is one.
- The relevant fields from the opportunity record.
- The approved demo scenarios, as an export with the status of each: available, limited or roadmap.

## What comes out

One plan in a fixed order: the items the pair must settle, an attendee brief, the buyer's workflow as stated, what the buyer has asked for and how each request matches an approved scenario, a timed demo sequence, open discovery questions, the setup each scenario needs, and a list of what is not to be shown or promised. It closes with a status line saying nothing has been sent, booked or changed. If the buyer has described no workflow, no sequence is built and the plan says so.

## What has to change for your company

Set up the approved demo scenario list first: one row for each scenario, with its status, its limits, the date it was last checked and its owner. Decide how recent that check must be, how many minutes the opening and the close take, and how many questions a plan may hold. Agree who confirms an unverified request, whether the solutions engineer or someone in product, and who answers price and terms. Name the opportunity record fields you will paste in, so the headings match every time.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
