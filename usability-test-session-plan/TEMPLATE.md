# Usability test session plan

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Turn a prototype or a confusing product flow into one plan for a round of usability sessions: the questions the product owner wants answered, the participant groups with a neutral question to check each criterion, a time budget for each session, realistic tasks written without the product's own button names, the only prompts the facilitator may use, a blank sheet for observation notes and the measures that will say whether people finished each task. Recording, consent, who sees the notes, how long they are kept and incentives are listed as decisions for a named person. The assistant prepares the plan. It invents no participant findings, recruits and contacts nobody, and judges no design. The research lead approves the method and recruitment, and the product owner chooses what is tested.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when a prototype or a flow exists, in clickable form or as a written step-by-step description, and the team wants to see whether people can finish a task with it before changing it.

Do not use it to make sense of conversations that have already happened (see customer interview synthesis), to write the pass and fail criteria for a story that is being built (see acceptance criteria sheet) or to prepare an acceptance test for an operational pilot (see pilot acceptance-test preparation). Do not use it to read results or to say which design is better; it makes no findings. It does not write the consent form, and it does not recruit anyone.

## Roles

- **Runs it and chooses what is tested:** the product owner.
- **Approves the method and the recruitment plan, and settles recording, consent, notes and retention:** the research lead.
- **Supplies the prototype and the words on each screen:** the designer, or the product owner where there is none.
- **Recruits, facilitates and takes notes on the day:** people the research lead names. The assistant does none of these.

## Inputs

Ask for them under these labels. If they arrive mixed together, ask which is which before starting.

1. **Prototype or flow description,** one screen or step per line, with the words on each screen. Default labels P1, P2 and so on; keep the owner's own labels if they have them. If missing, stop.
2. **Questions to answer,** one per line, chosen by the product owner. Default labels Q1, Q2. If missing, stop.
3. **Participant criteria,** grouped by who they apply to: groups labelled G1, G2 by default; "must have" criteria labelled U1, U2 and "must not have" criteria labelled M1, M2. If missing, continue and print "Not supplied: participant criteria"; the Participants section then says who cannot be chosen yet.
4. **Known pain points,** one per line, with where each came from (support requests, survey comments, observed behaviour). Default labels K1, K2. If missing, continue and say so.
5. **Constraints,** any of: sessions per group, session length in minutes, dates, remote or in person, language, who will facilitate and take notes, success targets, an upper limit on tasks. Where a number is missing, use the default below and mark it "(default)".
6. **Handling already decided,** such as the consent form and its owner, recording, test data, retention. If missing, every item in the decisions list is "Not decided".

These are free text, not an export, so there are no column headers to match. The labels above are the only thing to check for. If a screen, question, criterion or pain point carries no label, number it in the order given and show the numbering back on the plan.

## The procedure

Defaults, used only where the inputs do not say, and marked "(default)" wherever they appear: 5 sessions per group; 45 minutes per session; 5 minutes for the introduction and consent; 5 minutes for the wrap-up; a time cap of 5 minutes per task; a plan may fill at most 75% of the session; a pause of 20 seconds before the facilitator asks what the participant is thinking; 2 minutes without progress before the facilitator moves a participant on; a group whose planned time is under half the session length prompts a question; participant IDs R01, R02 and so on; an answer to an understanding question counted as names all the parties the question asks about, names some, or names none.

1. **Check the inputs.** Number them and count them for the "Inputs read" line. Stop if there is no prototype or flow description, or no question.
2. **Questions.** Keep the owner's questions as written. A question that no observed behaviour could answer (for example whether people like the design, or would pay for it) is listed under "Questions a task cannot answer", with the reason, and gets no task. Never reword a question to make it fit.
3. **Participants.** For each group, give the number of sessions and the criteria. Write one screening question for each criterion. A screening question is neutral: it is an open question about what the person does or has done, answered from their own experience, and it names neither the criterion nor the answer that qualifies. A yes-or-no question that names the criterion (for example "Do you work in software?") is not neutral, because it shows which answer qualifies; ask what the person does instead. State the qualifying answer beside it. A criterion that cannot be checked by asking (for example "good with technology") is listed under "Criteria that cannot be checked by a question"; never invent a way to check it. Never make up a quota, a mix or a number that the inputs and defaults do not give. Give each session a participant ID (default R01, R02 and so on, not the screen labels). Total the sessions.
4. **Tasks.** Write at least one task for every question that can be answered by observation, and a task for any known pain point that the owner wants covered. Each task has:
   - a group, and the question and pain points it answers;
   - a starting screen (a task that follows another starts on the screen where the earlier task ends, unless the inputs say how the participant gets elsewhere);
   - the exact words said to the participant, as a situation and a goal in the participant's own terms, not a list of steps;
   - a done condition that a person watching can see or hear;
   - the expected path through the screens;
   - a time cap;
   - what it depends on, if it starts from the result of an earlier task;
   - what must be prepared: the data the starting screen has to hold for the task to work (for example a waiting row), or "nothing".
   Check every task's wording against the words on the screens and the names the owner gave the screens. A task must not contain the label of a button, menu or screen title that the participant needs to find or use. Words for the product's ordinary subject matter may appear. List every label and screen name checked, and every word of a task that matches one. A word that is both ordinary subject matter and part of a label or title is reworded if it can be; if the task is clearer with it, keep it, say so in the check and ask the product owner to confirm. If a task cannot be worded without a label, rewrite it; if it still cannot, mark it and ask.
   Check each task that follows another against what the earlier screens and tasks already say. If a screen the participant has just seen states the answer to the later task's question, the later task may measure memory of that text more than understanding: say so as an open question.
   Check each task's starting screen against its question. A question about finding something is not answered by a task that starts on the screen that shows it. Start the task earlier; if the inputs do not describe the way in, say the task covers the question only in part and ask how the participant gets there.
5. **Time budget.** For each group: introduction, each task's cap, wrap-up, and the planned total against the session length and the limit on how much a plan may fill. Print the percentage to one decimal place when it is not a whole number, and the minutes unused. If the planned total is over the limit, do not shorten any task; print the figures, name the tasks, and ask the product owner which to move or drop. If a group's planned total is under half the session length, ask whether something should be added or the session shortened. Print the booked time: sessions times length. The caps cover the task itself; the end-of-task questions (N6, with the ease rating) are not in them and take their time from the unused minutes, and the plan says so.
6. **Neutral prompts.** Print the fixed set in the output format: the opening, thinking aloud, silence, "is this right", "what do I do", end of task (which includes the ease rating question) and wrap-up. The facilitator uses these words and no others. Never write a prompt that names a screen element, says whether something is right, or suggests what the participant should feel.
7. **Help rule.** Print the three levels in the output format and the rule that help is anything said beyond the fixed prompts. Level 2 has its own words for the last task of a session. Help is always written in the notes with the step it was given at.
8. **Observation note template.** Print the blank sheet from the output format, one set for each participant and task. It must keep what was seen and heard apart from what anyone thinks it means. Never fill it in.
9. **Success measures.** Define completed unaided, completed with help and not completed against each task's done condition, and name the measures recorded: time on task, wrong turns and an ease rating. Where a question is about understanding rather than doing, define how the answer will be counted from the participant's own words, with the default scale marked (default) unless the inputs give one. Define each result on the note sheet, including Stopped by participant. Targets come only from the inputs; if none are supplied, print "Targets: Not set" and ask. Never suggest what a good result is.
10. **Decisions.** Print the standing list: recording; consent wording; who may see the notes and recordings and for how long; test data; names in notes; incentive; language and access needs. Each has a named role as owner and either what the inputs settled or "Not decided". Defaults may be shown only where marked (default). Never write the consent wording or judge a company's data rules.
11. **Coverage and conflicts.** For every question, pain point and screen, list the tasks that touch it; print "No task" for any with none, and "in part" with the reason for a question a task answers only partly. If two inputs disagree (a criterion against a group, a constraint against another), list both with their labels and do not decide.
12. **Open questions.** Gather everything only a person can settle, one question each, numbered, with an owner. Include a task that depends on an earlier one when no prepared start state was supplied, a task that follows a screen already stating the answer, a task whose starting screen needs data the inputs do not say will be there, and any word kept in a task that is also part of a label.
13. **Print.** Use the output format exactly. Print "None." or "Not supplied" in any empty part. Leave the approvals empty.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No prototype or flow description, or no question:** stop and say which is missing.
- **A task cannot be worded without a button or screen label:** mark it, say which label, and ask.
- **The planned time is over the limit:** print the figures and ask; do not shorten tasks to fit.
- **The sessions asked for are more than the constraints allow:** print both numbers and ask; do not choose who is dropped.
- **Two inputs conflict:** list both; do not resolve.
- **A participant criterion cannot be checked by a question:** list it; never invent a check.
- **Real participant details, recordings, notes or results are pasted in:** do not use them. Say that the plan holds no participant findings and ask the person to remove them.
- **Asked to write the consent form, decide recording, fill in notes, report findings, say which design is better, recruit or contact anyone, or send the plan:** decline. The research lead and the product owner decide; people recruit and contact.
- **Asked for a password, login code or any access to the prototype beyond a link or description the person chooses to share:** never ask. Work from the description supplied.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every question has a task, or is listed as one a task cannot answer. (Product owner)
2. No task contains the label of a button, menu or screen title the participant must use. (Research lead)
3. Every participant criterion has a neutral screening question and a stated qualifying answer, or is listed as not checkable. (Research lead)
4. The time arithmetic is right: planned time, percentage, unused time and booked time. (Research lead)
5. The prompts are the fixed set and none leads the participant. (Research lead)
6. The note sheet is blank and keeps what happened apart from interpretation. (Research lead)
7. No target, quota or finding appears that the inputs did not supply. (Product owner)
8. Recording, consent, notes and retention are listed as decisions with an owner, and nothing is decided by the plan. (Research lead)
9. Every figure and default is marked or traceable to an input. (Product owner)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Normal plan.** Two groups, three tasks, 40-minute sessions, defaults for the rest. Expected: group of two tasks plans 20 of 40 minutes (50%); group of one task plans 15 of 40 (37.5%) and raises a question; booked time is sessions times 40.
2. **Task names a button.** A screen has a button labelled "Checkout" and a draft task says "Go to checkout". Expected: the check flags the word, the task is rewritten around the goal, and the result is printed.
3. **Over the limit.** Six tasks at the default cap in a 45-minute session. Expected: 5 + 30 + 5 = 40 minutes against a limit of 33.75 (75% of 45); nothing is shortened; the plan asks which tasks to move.
4. **Exactly at the limit.** Tasks and fixed parts total exactly 75% of the session. Expected: accepted with no question.
5. **Criterion that cannot be asked.** "Comfortable with technology". Expected: listed under criteria that cannot be checked by a question; no question invented.
6. **Question about liking.** "Do people like the new look?". Expected: listed under questions a task cannot answer, with the reason; no task written.
7. **No targets.** Expected: "Targets: Not set" and an open question.
8. **Findings pasted in.** The person pastes notes from a real session. Expected: not used; the plan says it holds no findings and asks for them to be removed.
9. **Asked to write the consent form or choose recording.** Expected: declined; the decision is listed with the research lead as owner.
10. **Dependent task with no start state.** Task 2 starts from the result of Task 1. Expected: an open question about a prepared state.
11. **Screener that gives the answer away.** A criterion "does not work in software". Expected: an open question about the person's field, with the disqualifying answer stated beside it; no yes-or-no question.
12. **Word that is subject matter and a label.** A task uses "shift" and a screen is titled "Shifts". Expected: the word is listed as matched, kept or reworded, and the product owner is asked to confirm if kept.
13. **Find question.** A question asks whether a manager can find a waiting request and the only task starts on the screen that lists it. Expected: covered in part, and an open question about the way in.
14. **Ease rating.** Expected: the rating question has fixed wording in N6, and no other question counts as help.

## What to customize

- Sessions per group, session length, the share of a session a plan may fill and the default time cap.
- The introduction, wrap-up and silence timings.
- Who recruits, who facilitates, who takes notes, and where the plan is stored.
- Your own consent form, recording policy, retention rules and incentive.
- The success targets the product owner is willing to be held to.
- The fixed prompts, if your research method has its own wording.
- Which products the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and which prototype links or files it may be given.
