# Usability test session plan

Before a prototype or a confusing flow goes in front of users, the research lead and product owner hold one plan: who to recruit, the tasks in plain words, the neutral prompts, a blank note sheet and how success is counted. Recording and data handling are listed as decisions.

*Workflow template · Software technology · Design and user experience · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/usability-test-session-plan/)*

## The outcome

The team stops arguing about a screen and starts watching people use it. The plan turns "we think the flow is confusing" into a few sessions with realistic tasks, the right participants, a script that does not lead anyone and a note sheet that keeps what happened apart from what anyone thinks it means.

Every question the product owner wants answered has a task behind it, and every known pain point is either covered or listed as not covered. Nothing is left to be improvised on the day, and nothing that needs a person's decision is quietly assumed.

## Who starts the work, and when

The product owner or the research lead starts it when a prototype is ready to put in front of users, or when a live flow keeps confusing people and the team wants to see why before changing the whole screen. The research lead approves the method and the recruitment plan. The product owner chooses what to test. The person who runs the sessions uses the plan on the day.

## What they supply

- The prototype or a description of the flow, step by step, including the words on each screen.
- The questions the product owner wants answered.
- Who the participants should be, and who must not be included.
- Known pain points, such as support requests or survey comments, with where they came from.
- The limits: number of sessions, session length, dates, remote or in person, language, who will watch and take notes.
- What the company has already decided about recording, consent and test data.

## What comes out

One plan in a fixed order: the questions, the participant groups with a neutral question to check each criterion, a time budget for each session, tasks written without the product's own button names, the prompts the facilitator may use, a blank observation note sheet, the success measures and a coverage check. Recording, consent, who sees the notes, how long they are kept and incentives are listed as decisions with an owner, marked "Not decided" until a person decides them.

## What has to change for your company

Set how many sessions you run per group, how long a session lasts, and how much of it the plan may fill. Replace the default time cap per task if your flows are longer. Add the targets the product owner is willing to be held to; the plan never invents them. Name who recruits, who facilitates and who takes notes, and where your consent form, recording policy and retention rules live. Keep your own research method if you have one and let the plan follow it.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
